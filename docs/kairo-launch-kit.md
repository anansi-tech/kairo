# Kairo Launch Kit — Outreach + Manager Letter

## 1. Validation message (step one — send to all 50 names)

Send via LinkedIn DM, text, or email depending on the relationship. Personalize the first line; keep the rest as-is.

> Hey [name] — quick one. I'm launching a 2-day hands-on AI workshop this fall: teaching working professionals to actually use AI in their jobs. Not theory — laptops open, and you leave with 2–3 working automations built on your real work.
>
> Before I open registration, I'm getting honest reactions from 20 people I respect. Could I grab 15 minutes this week to walk you through the outline and hear what you think?

Rules: never pitch the sale in this message. In the call, ask three questions — "Would this be useful to someone in your role?", "What would stop you from attending?", "Who else should I show this to?" The interested ones will ask about dates and price on their own. Those are your step-two list.

## 3. Manager justification one-pager

Save as PDF, attach to invites, and link from the site (`/manager-letter.pdf`).

---

**Professional Development Request: Kairo AI Fluency Workshop**

**What it is:** A 2-day hands-on workshop (Friday–Saturday, [DATES], Fort Lauderdale) teaching working professionals to use AI tools productively in their current role. Participants work on their own real job tasks throughout — the deliverable is 2–3 working AI automations in use by the following Monday, plus a one-page 30-day pilot plan for their team.

**Instructor:** David Noel — Senior AI Security Engineer at a Fortune 100 financial institution, where he built and operates the AI safety system governing enterprise-wide AI usage; adjunct CS professor at Nova Southeastern University; PhD dissertation committee member; founder of Anansi Technology. Published researcher on production LLM systems.

**What [employee] will bring back:**
- Working automations for their current responsibilities (drafting, analysis, reporting, document processing)
- A concrete 30-day plan for 3 AI pilots in their team, with expected time savings and identified risks
- Practical judgment on AI failure modes — hallucination, data leakage, prompt injection — taught by a practitioner who manages these risks at enterprise scale

**Why this format:** Hands-on cohort instruction (15 seats, instructor at each participant's laptop) rather than lecture or self-paced video. Alternating instruction and applied practice every 30–45 minutes, following established adult-learning research.

**Cost:** $1,995 founding-cohort rate (standard $3,000). Comparable university executive AI programs run $2,500–$10,000+ for less hands-on formats. One day of PTO required (Friday); no travel costs for South Florida employees.

**Follow-through:** 30-day post-workshop check-in, optional 1:1 office hours, and alumni group access are included.

## 4. Sequencing (four weeks)

- **Week 1:** Build the 50-name list. Send 15 validation messages. Book venue. Set dates. Point Claude Code at the site brief.
- **Week 2:** 10 more validation messages; hold the calls. Start LinkedIn posts (2–3/week — mini-lessons and build-in-public).
- **Week 3:** Send founding invites to warm leads. Host the free 60-minute preview session ("3 AI automations you can build this week" + live prompt-injection demo). Close with the founding offer.
- **Week 4:** Follow-ups. Second preview session if seats remain. Target: 10+ deposits.

**Kill/adjust signal:** if 30 conversations yield fewer than 8 committed deposits, the market is telling you something — revisit price, audience, or date before spending on venue extras.

## 5. Before anything goes public
Confirm American Express's outside-activity policy covers paid teaching. The manager letter above deliberately says "a Fortune 100 financial institution" rather than naming Amex — keep that framing everywhere until you have explicit clearance to do otherwise.
